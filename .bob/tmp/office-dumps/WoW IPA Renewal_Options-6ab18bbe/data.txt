How Savings Translate to 50% Efficiency
The ROI% and the efficiency% are different metrics — this is the narrative bridge connecting $1.46M Year 1 savings to hours of manual work released, mapping explicitly toward Woolworths' 50% target.
1
Establish the Manual Effort Baseline
~98% of Planning Analytics work is currently manual (workshop-confirmed). 750 users × ~40 hrs/week × 48 weeks = ~1,440,000 total annual labour hours in scope.
~1,411,200 hours/year of manual work — the efficiency% denominator [1]
2
Calculate Hours Released by AI in Year 1
Planning Analytics Agent: 375 active users × 4 hrs/week × 48 weeks = 72,000 gross hours released . IBM Bob: ~8,140 developer-hours of realised value (~$814K ÷ $100/hr).
Total gross hours released Year 1: ~80,140 hours [1]
3
Express as % of Manual Baseline
80,140 ÷ 1,411,200 — using 50% adoption, 15% realisation, and 25–30% conservative scaling.
Year 1: ~5.7% of total manual effort automated [1]
4
The 3-Year Trajectory to 50% — See Panel
Year 1 ramp → Year 2 adoption deepens → Year 3 full adoption + compounding modernisation flywheel. Note: 50% target applies to Planning Analytics; IBM Bob developer gains are additive upside.
$5.2M cumulative net return = the financial expression of this efficiency journey [1]
Why ROI% ≠ Efficiency%
ROI measures return on investment — a 537% ROI means every $1 invested returns $5.37. Efficiency% measures how much total manual workload AI has absorbed. These metrics move in the same direction but are calculated differently. [1]
